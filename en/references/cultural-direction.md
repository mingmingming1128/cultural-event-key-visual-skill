# Cultural evidence and direction

Read this reference when cultural source material, historical imagery, traditional art, religious content, regional identity, or concept exploration is involved.

## Separate evidence classes

- `RAW`: original place, object, performance, or documentary material.
- `TRD`: traditional, historical, museum, or archival material.
- `SEC`: contemporary reinterpretation.
- `COM`: commercial or brand reference.
- `LAY`: layout or editorial reference.
- `UNC`: unknown source or meaning.

Commercial work may teach a method but is not evidence of cultural meaning.

## Risk levels

- `L1`: usable as evidence or material within stated rights.
- `L2`: abstract first; use structure, rhythm, palette, or texture rather than literal copying.
- `L3`: verify meaning, rights, and context before use.
- `L4`: do not auto-use in public-facing work.

Treat unknown script, scripture, complete sacred figures, ritual diagrams, and ceremonial objects as L3 or L4 until verified.

## Visual-gene extraction

Record only genes that can guide design decisions:

- composition and negative-space behavior;
- scale, rhythm, repetition, and density;
- dominant, supporting, and accent color relationships;
- line, geometry, framing, and edge behavior;
- material, surface, grain, and light;
- spatial logic and movement;
- relationship between text and image;
- meanings that must not be distorted.

Translate genes into modern visual behavior. Avoid souvenir-like symbol piles, generic "traditional" decoration, and unsupported historical claims.

## Directions for ImageGen

Default to one strong direction and convert it directly into one high-fidelity ImageGen prompt. When exploration is explicitly requested, generate materially different high-fidelity territories rather than low-fidelity sketches:

1. cultural narrative;
2. contemporary translation;
3. spatial or experiential atmosphere.

Each direction states:

- communication idea;
- cultural evidence;
- memorable visual device;
- composition;
- palette and material;
- typography;
- audience/channel fit;
- cultural and production risks.

Reject a direction if replacing the event title would make it equally suitable for many unrelated events.
